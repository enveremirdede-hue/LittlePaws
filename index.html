Coexistence & Empathy

Why Protecting Street Animals Matters

Stray cats and dogs have shared urban environments with humans for centuries. Caring for them is a reflection of a civilized, compassionate, and healthy society.

Urban Ecological Balance

Street cats naturally control rodent and pest populations in urban centers, preventing vector-borne diseases without harsh toxic chemicals.

TNR & Health Management

Trap-Neuter-Return (TNR) alongside rabies vaccination programs keeps street animal populations stable, healthy, and friendly.

Community Empathy

Communities that foster kindness towards stray animals raise children with higher emotional intelligence, empathy, and social responsibility.

Seasonal Survival Support

Without human assistance, extreme winter freezing and summer dehydration cause severe suffering. Simple daily steps make survival possible.

Toxic vs. Safe Foods Checklist

NEVER FEED STRAY PETS:

  • Chocolate & Coffee: Contains toxic theobromine causing cardiac arrest.
  • Cooked Bones: Splinter easily and tear stomach/bowel lining.
  • Onions & Garlic: Destroys red blood cells causing severe anemia.
  • Cow's Milk: Stray cats/dogs are lactose intolerant; causes diarrhea & dehydration.

SAFE & NUTRITIOUS ALTERNATIVES:

  • Plain Unseasoned Boiled Chicken: Excellent digestible protein for weak strays.
  • Commercial Dry/Wet Food: Complete balanced nutrition formulated for cats/dogs.
  • Boiled Rice & Pumpkin: Helps settle digestive distress and upset stomach.
  • Clean Fresh Water: The #1 most critical resource for summer and winter survival!

DIY Styrofoam Shelter Blueprint

1. Obtain a heavy-duty styrofoam cooler box.
2. Cut a 15cm x 15cm round entrance hole on the side (away from wind).
3. Wrap exterior with a black heavy-duty trash bag to waterproof.
4. Fill inside with straw/hay or newspaper (Do NOT use blankets as they absorb moisture and freeze!).

Critical Winter Facts

Hypothermia Time Limit

In sub-zero temperatures, an unfed stray cat can succumb to hypothermia in as little as 6 hours, while a stray dog lasts 17 hours. High-calorie meals are vital for generating metabolic heat.

Pro Tip: Add a few drops of olive oil to water bowls to prevent fast freezing!

The 5-Second Asphalt Test

Press the back of your hand onto the asphalt for 5 seconds. If it's too hot for your hand, it will blister and tear stray dogs' and cats' paw pads! Keep feeding stations in shaded, grassy areas.

Ice-Cube Water Stations

Place wide, light-colored ceramic or plastic bowls in deep shade. Drop ice cubes into water bowls in the morning so the water stays cool throughout peak afternoon heat.

Summer Dehydration Warning

3 Days Without Water

Heatstroke and severe kidney failure are the leading causes of death for stray animals in summer. Placing a single bowl of clean water outside your home saves lives every day.
